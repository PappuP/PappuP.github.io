Ph.D. Candidate & Graduate Research Assistant
Department of Climate, Meteorology & Atmospheric Sciences
University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign

My research focuses on understanding the physical processes that control Earth's climate response to warming and improving how these processes are represented in Earth system models. Using climate model simulations, I investigate how sea surface temperature patterns, atmospheric circulation, clouds, and top-of-atmosphere radiation interact to shape climate feedbacks.

A major part of my research focuses on improving climate model parameterizations through Bayesian calibration, uncertainty quantification, and machine learning. I use statistical methods to constrain uncertain model parameters and investigate how high-fidelity cloud simulations can inform more accurate and computationally efficient cloud microphysics. My recent work includes developing neural-network corrections for cloud and rain processes while retaining the underlying physical framework.

I am Ph.D. candidate at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign and have been a member of CDDS@UIUC since Fall 2022, working with Dr. Cristian Proistosescu. My master's research focused on severe weather, particularly thunderstorms and lightning.

Outside of research, I enjoy playing and watching cricket, traveling, and cooking.

Research

Climate Feedbacks & Clouds

How tropical sea-surface warming patterns, atmospheric wave dynamics, and low clouds shape the radiative response to warming.

Earth System Model Calibration

Objective parameter optimization and uncertainty quantification, including calibration with single-column models.

AI/ML for the Earth System

Machine-learning corrections to physical parameterizations, such as warm-cloud sedimentation, and ML emulators of global atmosphere models.

Severe Weather

Thunderstorms, lightning, and heatwaves over Bangladesh using the WRF-ARW model and lightning potential diagnostics.
